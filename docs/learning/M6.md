# M6: the backtester

Written when the code was complete (2026-10-04). The G2 run waits for bars,
events and the revenue feature; its report is `reports/M6_backtest.md`.

## What was built, and why this design

A backtest that flatters a strategy is worse than none, because it is
believed. Every choice in M6 leans against the strategy.

- **Costs from official sources.** Brokerage, STT, stamp duty, exchange
  charges, SEBI fees, GST and DP charges, each with its circular, effective
  dates and the date it was checked. The model refuses a date it has no
  verified rates for instead of guessing.
- **Strategies are pure.** A strategy maps what it is shown (an event, a
  closed bar, its positions) to signals. No I/O, no hidden state. That is
  what lets the same object run in a backtest, in paper trading and live.
- **A pessimistic engine.** An order reaches the market after a delay and
  can only fill in a bar that *starts* later; it fills at most a share of
  that bar's volume, with slippage; a gap through its protection band means
  no fill; no buying into an upper circuit; intraday positions are squared
  off; delivery sales settle the next day; no leverage.
- **A risk engine with a veto.** Every order passes fixed rules (position
  size, daily loss, liquidity, surveillance lists, order rate, stale data,
  a kill switch) or is refused with the one rule it broke. Missing
  information is a refusal.
- **A ledger that reconciles.** Fills are matched first-in-first-out into
  trades, and the cash implied by the trades must equal the engine's cash
  to the paisa. If it does not, something is wrong and the run says so.
- **A registry written before the run.** Each backtest is logged before it
  starts, so abandoned and failed designs are counted too. The count feeds
  the deflated Sharpe ratio.

## Key concepts

- **Backtest overfitting.** Try enough variations and one looks good by
  chance. The deflated Sharpe ratio asks how good the best of N trials would
  look if none had any edge, and discounts for it.
- **Walk-forward with an embargo.** Train on the past, test on the future,
  and leave a gap between them so information cannot leak across the border.
- **Leak detection by perturbation.** Change the data after a decision
  point; if any earlier decision changes, the code looked ahead.
- **Capacity.** A strategy that needs 40% of a stock's daily volume does
  not exist at that size.

## What could go wrong

- One-minute bars say nothing about the order book: real slippage can only
  be measured with real orders (M8).
- The fill model's parameters (participation, impact) are assumptions; G3
  compares paper against the backtest, not against the market.
- Costs change by circular. A rate that is right today is wrong after the
  next revision unless the table gets a new period.
